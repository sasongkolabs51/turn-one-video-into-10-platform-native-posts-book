# 10.9 Discuss: Book Club and Group Prompts

**How to run this session:** This 45-minute book club session uses two warm-up prompts, six core prompts, and two closing prompts. Spend two minutes on warm-ups and closings, and four minutes on core topics. When disagreements arise about serialization depth, encourage participants to share specific examples of audience fatigue rather than debating abstract content calendars.

### 10.9.1 Warm-up

#### 10.9.1.1 Prompt chaining

*What stood out to you about breaking long-form content creation into three sequential prompts instead of asking the AI to write it all at once?*

*Facilitator note:* Start with a quick round-robin to lower the barrier and get everyone sharing their initial reactions.

#### 10.9.1.2 Improv mindset

*Which part of treating the AI as a creative improv partner using the "yes, and" technique feels the most different from your current editing process?*

*Facilitator note:* Invite volunteers to share their answers to build momentum without pressuring quieter members.

### 10.9.2 Core

#### 10.9.2.1 Serialization fatigue

*Where might forcing a single pillar into a rigid Five-Day Content Arc actually fatigue your audience if the core idea lacks enough depth for a week of posts?*

*Facilitator note:* Listen for whether participants distinguish between natural narrative expansion and artificially stretching thin content just to fill a schedule.

#### 10.9.2.2 Skipping the check

*In what specific scenarios would running the Pre-Deepening Quality Check actually slow down your workflow without catching any meaningful structural or voice errors?*

*Facilitator note:* Pay attention to whether the group recognizes that highly constrained, short-form outputs might not require the same rigorous checklist as long-form essays.

#### 10.9.2.3 Intellectual tension

*How does the Integration Quality Check ensure that the AI preserves your intellectual tension rather than smoothing away your contrarian thoughts into generic agreement?*

*Facilitator note:* Encourage the group to explore the danger of AI defaulting to safe, universally acceptable statements that dilute your unique perspective.

#### 10.9.2.4 Catching drift

*What specific subtle signs of voice drift are you most likely to miss when reading a batch of generated pieces in sequence without a dedicated audit checklist?*

*Facilitator note:* If the discussion stalls, ask the group to share an example of a time a creator's tone slowly shifted without them noticing.

#### 10.9.2.5 Placeholder anecdotes

*Why is it more effective to have the AI insert placeholder anecdotes during the drafting phase rather than attempting to write the perfect personal story upfront?*

*Facilitator note:* Guide the conversation toward how separating structural drafting from personal storytelling prevents cognitive overload and perfectionism.

#### 10.9.2.6 Emotional roles

*How does assigning distinct emotional and structural roles to each day of your arc prevent your multiplatform content from feeling repetitive to your core audience?*

*Facilitator note:* Listen for insights on how varying the emotional function keeps the audience engaged while reinforcing the central pillar message.

### 10.9.3 Closing

#### 10.9.3.1 Tracking friction

*Which specific link in your prompt chain do you suspect will create the most friction, and how will you use the timing log to fix it?*

*Facilitator note:* Invite volunteers to share their suspected bottlenecks to land the session with a concrete commitment.

#### 10.9.3.2 First chain

*What high-scoring pillar piece will you select for your very first Three-Link Amplification Chain to ensure your output is unmistakably you?*

*Facilitator note:* Ask participants to name one actionable takeaway to ensure the group leaves with a clear, forward-looking focus.

### 10.9.4 If the group is quiet during Warm-up
- Ask a simple yes/no question about whether they have ever felt AI lost their voice in long outputs, then invite elaboration.
- Share a brief, relatable example of a time an AI completely missed the emotional nuance of a personal story.

### 10.9.5 If the group is quiet during Core
- Offer a hypothetical scenario where a creator tries to stretch a simple tip into a five-day narrative arc and the audience unsubscribes.
- Ask the group to identify which day of the Five-Day Arc they find most difficult to conceptualize.

### 10.9.6 If the group is quiet during Closing
- Ask participants to write down their chosen pillar piece on a slip of paper and share it with the person next to them.
- Invite the group to collectively name the biggest benefit of treating AI as an improv partner.

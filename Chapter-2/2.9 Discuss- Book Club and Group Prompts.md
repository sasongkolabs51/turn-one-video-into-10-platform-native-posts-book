# 2.9 Discuss: Book Club and Group Prompts

**How to run this session:** This 45-minute session includes two warm-up prompts, six core prompts, and two closing prompts. Spend about two minutes on warm-ups and closings, and four minutes on each core prompt. When disagreements arise about AI authenticity, guide the group back to the audience experience framework.

### 2.9.1 Warm-up

#### 2.9.1.1 First impressions

*Which of the four mindset shifts felt the most surprising or counterintuitive when you first read about it in the chapter?*

*Facilitator note:* Start with a quick round-robin to lower the barrier and get everyone speaking early.

#### 2.9.1.2 Past frustrations

*What was your immediate emotional reaction the first time an AI tool completely missed your personal voice and produced generic sludge?*

*Facilitator note:* Invite volunteers to share a brief, funny, or frustrating anecdote to build rapport.

### 2.9.2 Core

#### 2.9.2.1 Documenting voice

*When breaking your voice into five mechanical dimensions, which specific dimension seems the most difficult to document and teach to a machine?*

*Facilitator note:* Listen for whether participants struggle more with structural elements like hooks or abstract elements like emotional register.

#### 2.9.2.2 Rigid boundaries

*The chapter argues for a strict division of labor where the AI never invents core ideas; where might this rigid boundary actually limit creative serendipity?*

*Facilitator note:* Listen for whether participants separate the framework from the outcome; if not, ask what changes when they do.

#### 2.9.2.3 Delegating comfort

*If you fully adopt the Cocreator Pact, what specific part of your current creative workflow will feel the most uncomfortable to delegate?*

*Facilitator note:* Pay attention to underlying fears about losing control or losing the joy of the creative process.

#### 2.9.2.4 Future drafting

*How might the process of extracting your Voice Fingerprint change the way you write your original hub content in the future?*

*Facilitator note:* Probe for insights about how analyzing past work creates new rules for future drafting.

#### 2.9.2.5 Breaking points

*Under what specific conditions would the Authenticity Reframe Practice fail to salvage a draft, forcing you to start over manually?*

*Facilitator note:* Ask the group to identify the exact breaking point where structural editing becomes more work than rewriting.

#### 2.9.2.6 Audience expectations

*The chapter defines authenticity as audience experience rather than manual typing; how does this definition hold up when your audience explicitly expects raw, unedited human effort?*

*Facilitator note:* Encourage the group to explore the tension between scalable systems and audience expectations for vulnerability.

### 2.9.3 Closing

#### 2.9.3.1 Enforcing rules

*What is one specific boundary rule from your Cocreator Pact that you will enforce during your very next AI interaction?*

*Facilitator note:* Ask each participant to name one action they will take before the next session.

#### 2.9.3.2 Measuring success

*How will you measure your success as an editorial orchestrator at the end of next week instead of counting words typed?*

*Facilitator note:* Land the session by having the group share one concrete metric they will track.

### 2.9.4 If the group is quiet during Warm-up
- Ask a yes/no question first, then invite elaboration.
- Share your own brief AI burn moment to model vulnerability.

### 2.9.5 If the group is quiet during Core
- Offer a hypothetical scenario drawn from the chapter.
- Ask participants to write down their thought for one minute before sharing.

### 2.9.6 If the group is quiet during Closing
- Ask each participant to name one word that captures their takeaway.
- Provide a specific example of a boundary rule to spark ideas.

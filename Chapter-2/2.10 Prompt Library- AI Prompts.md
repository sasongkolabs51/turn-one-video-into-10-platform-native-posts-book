# 2.10 Prompt Library: AI Prompts

### 2.10.1 Voice-Constrained Generation Prompt

**When to use this:** Use this when you need to test your documented voice patterns against a raw piece of hub content.

**Expected output:** A rewritten draft that strictly adheres to your specific hook, rhythm, vocabulary, and tone constraints.

```text
You are a structural editor who writes in my voice according to the constraints below.

Task: Rewrite the content below as a [TARGET_PLATFORM] post. Follow every voice rule exactly.

Voice Rules:
- Hook style: [HOOK_PATTERN]
- Sentence rhythm: [RHYTHM_PATTERN]
- Lexical fingerprints to include: [LEXICAL_FINGERPRINTS]
- Argument structure: [ARGUMENT_STRUCTURE]
- Emotional register: [EMOTIONAL_TONE]

Content:
[HUB_CONTENT]

Before generating the final output, ask clarifying questions if any of my constraints or the content are ambiguous. If you do not know how to adapt a specific fact or context based on my rules, say "I don't know" rather than inventing facts, statistics, or citations.
```

**How to adapt:** Start by replacing the `[TARGET_PLATFORM]` and `[HUB_CONTENT]` placeholders with your specific channel and raw text.

### 2.10.2 Cocreator Pact Definition

**When to use this:** Use this to establish a clear division of labor before starting a new AI-assisted content project.

**Expected output:** A structured operating charter detailing your inputs, the AI's execution boundaries, and failure modes.

```text
Act as an editorial operations consultant. I need to draft a Cocreator Pact for a new content project. 

Here is the project context: [PROJECT_CONTEXT]
Here is the target platform: [TARGET_PLATFORM]

Based on the Cocreator Pact framework, generate a division of labor table that includes:
1. Primary Contribution (Creator vs. AI)
2. Core Input (What I provide vs. What AI provides)
3. Boundary Rules (What I must never delegate vs. What AI must never override)
4. Failure Modes (How vague briefs hurt vs. How AI overstepping hurts)

Before generating the table, ask clarifying questions if my project context is ambiguous. If you lack specific industry knowledge about my niche, say "I don't know" rather than inventing facts, statistics, or citations.
```

**How to adapt:** Fill in the `[PROJECT_CONTEXT]` with a brief description of your pillar content or campaign goal.

### 2.10.3 Voice Fingerprint Audit

**When to use this:** Use this to analyze a piece of your past writing and extract your mechanical voice dimensions.

**Expected output:** A breakdown of your hook architecture, sentence rhythm, lexical fingerprints, argument structure, and emotional register.

```text
Act as a forensic linguist. I want to extract my Voice Fingerprint from a sample of my best-performing content.

Here is the text sample: [TEXT_SAMPLE]

Analyze the text and extract the following five mechanical dimensions:
1. Hook Architecture (How I typically open)
2. Sentence Rhythm & Length (My cadence and pacing)
3. Lexical Fingerprints (Words, phrases, or colloquialisms I overuse)
4. Argument Structure (How I build my case logically)
5. Emotional Register (My typical tone and vulnerability level)

Provide concrete examples from the text for each dimension. 

Before analyzing, ask clarifying questions if the text sample is ambiguous or too short. If you cannot determine a specific dimension from the text provided, say "I don't know" rather than inventing patterns, facts, or citations.
```

**How to adapt:** Paste a transcript or high-performing post into the `[TEXT_SAMPLE]` placeholder to get the most accurate baseline.

### 2.10.4 Orchestrator Workflow Plan

**When to use this:** Use this to plan your weekly content workflow as an editorial orchestrator rather than a manual writer.

**Expected output:** A step-by-step schedule that separates your high-level editorial duties from the AI's structural execution.

```text
Act as a productivity and workflow coach for content creators. I am shifting from a manual writer to an editorial orchestrator using AI tools.

My weekly content goal is: [WEEKLY_GOAL]
My available working hours for this are: [AVAILABLE_HOURS]

Create a step-by-step weekly workflow plan that clearly separates my human tasks (creative briefs, voice constraints, final editorial judgment) from the AI's tasks (structural adaptation, formatting, first-draft generation). Include time estimates for each phase.

Before creating the plan, ask clarifying questions if my goals or hours are ambiguous. If you do not know the typical time requirements for my specific content format, say "I don't know" rather than inventing facts, statistics, or citations.
```

**How to adapt:** Adjust the `[WEEKLY_GOAL]` to reflect the exact number of platforms or assets you intend to publish.

### 2.10.5 AI Burn Moment Diagnosis

**When to use this:** Use this to diagnose why a recent AI-generated draft felt generic, off-brand, or disappointing.

**Expected output:** A diagnostic breakdown identifying the missing constraints and a revised prompt strategy to prevent the failure next time.

```text
Act as an AI prompt engineer and diagnostic consultant. I recently experienced an "AI burn moment" where the output was disappointing.

Here is what I asked the AI to do: [ORIGINAL_PROMPT]
Here is what felt "off" about the output: [OUTPUT_FAILURE]

Diagnose the failure by identifying the missing structural, voice, or context constraints. Then, provide a revised prompt template that includes the necessary constraints to prevent this specific failure mode in the future.

Before diagnosing, ask clarifying questions if my description of the failure is ambiguous. If you cannot determine the exact cause without more context, say "I don't know" rather than inventing facts, statistics, or citations.
```

**How to adapt:** Be as specific as possible in the `[OUTPUT_FAILURE]` placeholder about whether the issue was tone, structure, or factual hallucination.

### 2.10.6 Voice Fingerprint Extension

**When to use this:** Use this to adapt your documented written Voice Fingerprint into constraints for a new medium like video scripts or audio show notes.

**Expected output:** A translated set of voice rules optimized for spoken word or a different structural format.

```text
Act as a cross-media content strategist. I have a documented Voice Fingerprint for my written content, and I need to adapt it for a new medium.

My current written voice rules are: [WRITTEN_VOICE_RULES]
The new target medium is: [TARGET_MEDIUM]

Translate my written voice rules into structural constraints suitable for the target medium. Explain how my hook architecture, sentence rhythm, and emotional register should shift to fit this new format while remaining authentic to my core identity.

Before translating, ask clarifying questions if my current rules or the target medium are ambiguous. If you do not know the specific narrative grammar of the target medium, say "I don't know" rather than inventing facts, statistics, or citations.
```

**How to adapt:** Change the `[TARGET_MEDIUM]` to something specific like "short-form vertical video" or "long-form podcast interview."

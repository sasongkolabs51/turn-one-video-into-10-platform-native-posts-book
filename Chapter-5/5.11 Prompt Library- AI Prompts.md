# 5.11 Prompt Library: AI Prompts

### 5.11.1 Twitter Thread Voice-Preserving Prompt

**When to use this:** When you need to turn a Hub module into a high-tension, curiosity-driven thread.

**Expected output:** A formatted Twitter thread with rapid line breaks and curiosity gaps.

```text
Act as an expert ghostwriter. I will provide my Voice Constraints and a Hub module. Write a Twitter thread that strictly follows my Voice Constraints. Structural Rules: Use short, punchy sentences. Create curiosity gaps at the end of the first three tweets to encourage reading further. Use rapid line-break cadence. Do not use hashtags unless explicitly requested. Include a strong hook in the first tweet and a reflective question in the final tweet. Here are my Voice Constraints: [VOICE_CONSTRAINTS]. Here is the Hub module: [HUB_MODULE]. Ask clarifying questions before answering if the reader's input is ambiguous. Say "I don't know" rather than invent facts, statistics, or citations.
```

**How to adapt:** Paste your condensed voice rules into [VOICE_CONSTRAINTS] and your source text into [HUB_MODULE].

### 5.11.5 Output Scoring & Diagnosis

**When to use this:** When an AI generation feels slightly off and you need to quantify exactly why.

**Expected output:** A scored evaluation of the text with specific recommendations for prompt refinement.

```text

Evaluate the draft using the Prompt Scoring Rubric across two dimensions: Voice Accuracy (1-5) and Format Correctness (1-5). Provide the numerical scores, justify them by pointing out specific words or structural failures, and suggest one exact constraint to add to my prompt to improve the score. Here is my Voice Fingerprint: [VOICE_FINGERPRINT]. Here are the Structural Rules: [STRUCTURAL_RULES]. Here is the draft: [AI_DRAFT]. Ask clarifying questions before answering if the reader's input is ambiguous. Say "I don't know" rather than invent facts, statistics, or citations.
```

**How to adapt:** Paste the raw AI output into [AI_DRAFT] and your platform rules into [STRUCTURAL_RULES].

### 5.11.7 Prompt Failure Troubleshooter

**When to use this:** When your AI outputs consistently fail the scoring rubric due to common drift patterns.

**Expected output:** A diagnosis of the specific failure mode and a rewritten constraint block to fix it.

```text
Act as an AI troubleshooting expert. My Voice-Preserving Prompt is failing. The AI output is exhibiting the following symptom: [FAILURE_SYMPTOM]. Diagnose whether this is Generic Opening Creep, Format Bleed, or Data Hallucination. Explain why my current prompt is allowing this drift, and write a single, strict negative constraint or structural rule I can append to my prompt to permanently block this behavior. Here is my current prompt: [CURRENT_PROMPT]. Here is the failing output: [FAILING_OUTPUT]. Ask clarifying questions before answering if the reader's input is ambiguous. Say "I don't know" rather than invent facts, statistics, or citations.
```

**How to adapt:** Describe the exact error (e.g., "invented a statistic" or "sounded like a corporate press release") in [FAILURE_SYMPTOM].

### 5.11.8 Cross-Medium Voice Adapter

**Expected output:** A modified Voice Constraints block optimized for spoken cadence and pacing.

```text
Act as a cross-media content adapter. I have a dense Voice Constraints block designed for written text. I need to adapt it for a spoken medium: [SPOKEN_MEDIUM]. Rewrite my Voice Constraints block to preserve my core identity, argument structure, and signature phrases, but adjust the mechanical rules for spoken cadence, breath pauses, and conversational transitions. Output the new, adapted Voice Constraints block ready to be pasted into a scriptwriting prompt. Here is my written Voice Constraints block: [WRITTEN_VOICE_CONSTRAINTS]. Ask clarifying questions before answering if the reader's input is ambiguous. Say "I don't know" rather than invent facts, statistics, or citations.
```

**How to adapt:** Specify the target spoken format, such as "podcast intro" or "YouTube video essay," in [SPOKEN_MEDIUM].

# 5.10 Discuss: Book Club and Group Prompts

**How to run this session:** This 45-minute book club session uses two warm-up prompts, six core prompts, and two closing prompts. Spend two minutes on warm-ups and closings, and four minutes on core topics. When disagreements arise about scoring rubrics or AI constraints, encourage participants to share specific examples of prompt failures rather than debating abstract theory.

### 5.10.1 Warm-up

#### 5.10.1.1 First impressions

*Which of the five prompt components surprised you the most when you realized it was missing from your previous AI interactions?*

*Facilitator note:* Start with a quick round-robin to lower the barrier and get everyone sharing their initial reactions.

#### 5.10.1.2 Platform differences

*What specific structural rule for Twitter threads stood out to you as the hardest to enforce using AI without explicit constraints?*

*Facilitator note:* Invite volunteers to share their answers to build momentum without pressuring quieter members.

### 5.10.2 Core

#### 5.10.2.1 Scoring subjectivity

*Where might relying on a one-to-five scoring rubric for Voice Accuracy introduce personal bias that actually hinders your prompt refinement process?*

*Facilitator note:* Listen for whether participants recognize that subjective scoring can lead to inconsistent iterations if the rubric criteria aren't strictly defined.

#### 5.10.2.2 Testing rigor

*Why does the chapter require running each prompt three times to establish a baseline instead of trusting the first highly successful output?*

*Facilitator note:* Pay attention to whether the group distinguishes between AI's occasional lucky guesses and consistent, reliable performance across multiple generations.

#### 5.10.2.3 Swap test failures

*In what specific scenarios would a prompt that perfectly passes the initial test completely fail the Swap Test when applied to a new Hub?*

*Facilitator note:* Encourage the group to explore edge cases where prompts rely too heavily on the specific topic rather than generalized structural rules.

#### 5.10.2.4 Voice constraints

*What specific challenges arise when you try to condense your unique Voice Fingerprint into a single, dense paragraph of static constraints?*

*Facilitator note:* If the discussion stalls, ask the group to share an example of a voice trait that is nearly impossible to explain in plain text.

#### 5.10.2.5 Iteration loop

*How does adding a single targeted constraint during the iteration loop prevent the prompt from becoming an unreadable wall of conflicting instructions?*

*Facilitator note:* Listen for insights on how incremental changes help isolate the exact variable that fixed a Voice Accuracy or Format Correctness failure.

#### 5.10.2.6 Format bleed

*What specific structural grammar rules for LinkedIn carousels are most frequently ignored by AI when generating slide scripts without strict formatting constraints?*

*Facilitator note:* Guide the conversation toward practical examples of format bleed, such as the AI writing long paragraphs instead of punchy slide text.

### 5.10.3 Closing

#### 5.10.3.1 Playbook integration

*What is the most significant friction point you anticipate when attempting to integrate your newly tested prompts directly into your daily Platform-Format Playbook?*

*Facilitator note:* Invite volunteers to share their integration challenges to land the session with practical, forward-looking problem-solving.

#### 5.10.3.2 Library readiness

*Which specific platform's prompt template will you finalize first to ensure your core library is ready for your next content sprint?*

*Facilitator note:* Ask participants to name one actionable takeaway to ensure the group leaves with a clear, forward-looking focus.

### 5.10.4 If the group is quiet during Warm-up
- Ask a simple yes/no question about whether they have ever been frustrated by generic AI outputs, then invite elaboration.
- Share a brief, relatable example of a time a prompt completely ignored your formatting instructions.

### 5.10.5 If the group is quiet during Core
- Offer a hypothetical scenario where a creator tries to score a highly robotic AI output using the Voice Accuracy rubric.
- Ask the group to identify which of the five prompt components they find most difficult to write.

### 5.10.6 If the group is quiet during Closing
- Ask participants to write down their chosen platform on a slip of paper and share it with the person next to them.
- Invite the group to collectively name the biggest benefit of having a fully tested prompt library.

# 9.10 Discuss: Book Club and Group Prompts

**How to run this session:** This 45-minute book club session uses two warm-up prompts, six core prompts, and two closing prompts. Spend two minutes on warm-ups and closings, and four minutes on core topics. When disagreements arise about scoring dimensions, encourage participants to share specific examples of past content misalignments rather than debating abstract metrics.

### 9.10.1 Warm-up

#### 9.10.1.1 First impressions

*What specific dimension of the Repurposing Decision Matrix—evergreen strength, emotional resonance, or shareability—made you rethink how you currently evaluate your own content?*

*Facilitator note:* Start with a quick round-robin to lower the barrier and get everyone sharing their initial reactions.

#### 9.10.1.2 Platform goals

*Which platform's primary business goal felt the most difficult to define clearly when you first looked at the Metrics-and-Goals Map?*

*Facilitator note:* Invite volunteers to share their answers to build momentum without pressuring quieter members.

### 9.10.2 Core

#### 9.10.2.1 Past misalignments

*How did sorting your last ten pillar pieces reveal that your past repurposing efforts were driven more by recency bias than by your actual strategic priorities?*

*Facilitator note:* Listen for whether participants recognize the difference between gut feelings and data-informed resource allocation.

#### 9.10.2.2 Questioning the matrix

*Where might relying strictly on a composite score from the Repurposing Decision Matrix cause you to completely ignore a timely, news-jacked piece of content that deserves immediate attention?*

*Facilitator note:* Pay attention to whether the group distinguishes between evergreen strategy and the need for agile, reactive content creation.

#### 9.10.2.3 Editorial instinct

*What specific tension arises when the matrix recommends heavily repurposing a vulnerable piece, but your editorial instinct strongly advises caution regarding audience reception?*

*Facilitator note:* Encourage the group to explore how conditional logic and tone adjustments can resolve the conflict between data and empathy.

#### 9.10.2.4 When maintenance fails

*In what specific scenarios would a monthly maintenance ritual fail to correct metric myopia if your underlying business goals have fundamentally shifted since you built the matrix?*

*Facilitator note:* Guide the conversation toward the danger of optimizing for outdated vanity metrics instead of current business outcomes.

#### 9.10.2.5 AI scoring limits

*Why might an AI-generated metadata header consistently misjudge the emotional resonance of a highly nuanced pillar piece compared to a human evaluating the same content?*

*Facilitator note:* If the discussion stalls, ask the group to share an example of a time an AI completely missed the emotional subtext of a text.

#### 9.10.2.6 The no-data trap

*How would you design a Discovery Sprint for a brand-new pillar piece to ensure it escapes the no-data trap without wasting your entire weekly sprint budget?*

*Facilitator note:* Listen for insights on how to balance testing new content with maximizing the return on proven, high-scoring assets.

### 9.10.3 Closing

#### 9.10.3.1 Next sprint focus

*Which specific high-scoring pillar piece will you select for your next repurposing sprint, and what platform goal will it specifically target?*

*Facilitator note:* Invite volunteers to share their chosen pillar piece to land the session with a concrete commitment.

#### 9.10.3.2 First maintenance step

*What is the very first metric or weighting you will adjust during your next monthly maintenance ritual to ensure your matrix reflects your true business priorities?*

*Facilitator note:* Ask participants to name one actionable takeaway to ensure the group leaves with a clear, forward-looking focus.

### 9.10.4 If the group is quiet during Warm-up
- Ask a simple yes/no question about whether they currently track metrics for their posts, then invite elaboration.
- Share a brief, relatable example of a time you heavily repurposed a piece that completely flopped.

### 9.10.5 If the group is quiet during Core
- Offer a hypothetical scenario where a creator spends hours repurposing a highly shareable post that generates zero leads.
- Ask the group to identify which of the three matrix dimensions they find most difficult to score objectively.

### 9.10.6 If the group is quiet during Closing
- Ask participants to write down their chosen high-scoring pillar piece on a slip of paper and share it with the person next to them.
- Invite the group to collectively name the biggest benefit of replacing gut feelings with a data-informed matrix.

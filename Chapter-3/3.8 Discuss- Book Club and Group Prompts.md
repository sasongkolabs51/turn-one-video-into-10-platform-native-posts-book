# 3.8 Discuss: Book Club and Group Prompts

**How to run this session:** This 45-minute session includes two warm-up prompts, six core prompts, and two closing prompts. Spend about two minutes on warm-ups and closings, and four minutes on each core prompt. When disagreements arise about platform rules, guide the group back to the audience experience framework.

### 3.8.1 Warm-up

#### 3.8.1.1 First impressions

*Which platform's native grammar felt the most surprising or counterintuitive when you first read about it in the chapter?*

*Facilitator note:* Start with a quick round-robin to lower the barrier and get everyone speaking early.

#### 3.8.1.2 Past frustrations

*What was your immediate reaction to the idea that simply summarizing pillar content for every channel is actually a flawed approach?*

*Facilitator note:* Invite volunteers to share a brief, funny, or frustrating anecdote about past cross-posting failures to build rapport.

### 3.8.2 Core

#### 3.8.2.1 Documenting grammar

*When breaking down platform grammars, which specific structural rule seems the most difficult to document and teach to an AI tool?*

*Facilitator note:* Listen for whether participants struggle more with structural elements like hooks or abstract elements like emotional cadence.

#### 3.8.2.2 Rigid boundaries

*The chapter argues for codifying your personal hook patterns and cadence rules; where might this rigid documentation actually limit creative serendipity?*

*Facilitator note:* Listen for whether participants separate the framework from the outcome; if not, ask what changes when they do.

#### 3.8.2.3 Systematizing comfort

*If you fully adopt the Platform-Format Playbook, what specific part of your current creative workflow will feel the most uncomfortable to systematize?*

*Facilitator note:* Pay attention to underlying fears about losing control or losing the spontaneous joy of the creative process.

#### 3.8.2.4 Future drafting

*How might the process of mapping a single insight side-by-side for two platforms change the way you draft your original pillar content?*

*Facilitator note:* Probe for insights about how analyzing past work creates new structural rules for future drafting.

#### 3.8.2.5 Breaking points

*Under what specific conditions would the translation mindset fail to salvage a piece of content, forcing you to start over from scratch?*

*Facilitator note:* Ask the group to identify the exact breaking point where structural rearchitecture becomes more work than rewriting.

#### 3.8.2.6 Audience expectations

*The chapter defines repurposing as an act of structural translation; how does this definition hold up when your audience explicitly expects raw, unedited human effort?*

*Facilitator note:* Encourage the group to explore the tension between scalable systems and audience expectations for vulnerability.

### 3.8.3 Closing

#### 3.8.3.1 Enforcing rules

*What is one specific structural rule from your Platform-Format Playbook that you will include in your very next AI prompt?*

*Facilitator note:* Ask each participant to name one action they will take before the next session.

#### 3.8.3.2 Measuring success

*How will you measure your success as a platform-native storyteller at the end of next week using qualitative audience feedback?*

*Facilitator note:* Land the session by having the group share one concrete metric they will track.

### 3.8.4 If the group is quiet during Warm-up
- Ask a yes/no question first, then invite elaboration.
- Share your own brief cross-posting failure to model vulnerability.

### 3.8.5 If the group is quiet during Core
- Offer a hypothetical scenario drawn from the chapter.
- Ask participants to write down their thought for one minute before sharing.

### 3.8.6 If the group is quiet during Closing
- Ask each participant to name one word that captures their takeaway.
- Provide a specific example of a platform rule to spark ideas.

# 3.9 Prompt Library: AI Prompts

### 3.9.1 AI Platform-Brief Simulation

**When to use this:** Use this when you need to transform a piece of Hub content into a highly specific, platform-native draft using your documented rules.

**Expected output:** A rearchitected draft that strictly follows your platform's structural grammar and your personal voice constraints.

```text
You are an editorial assistant trained in my voice and the following platform rules for [TARGET_PLATFORM]:

[PLATFORM_PLAYBOOK_RULES]

Using the Hub content below, generate a [CONTENT_TYPE] that opens with [HOOK_STYLE], teaches [CORE_INSIGHT], and ends with [CLOSING_MOVE].

Hub Content:
[HUB_CONTENT]

Before generating the final output, ask clarifying questions if my constraints or the content are ambiguous. If you do not know how to adapt a specific fact or context based on my rules, say "I don't know" rather than inventing facts, statistics, or citations.
```

**How to adapt:** Start by pasting your specific Platform-Format Playbook rules into the `[PLATFORM_PLAYBOOK_RULES]` placeholder.

### 3.9.2 Platform Grammar Audit

**When to use this:** Use this to analyze a piece of your past content and extract the structural rules that made it successful on a specific platform.

**Expected output:** A breakdown of the hook, cadence, and visual cues that define your native grammar for that channel.

```text
Act as a forensic content strategist. I want to extract my native structural grammar from a sample of my best-performing content on [TARGET_PLATFORM].

Here is the text sample:
[TEXT_SAMPLE]

Analyze the text and extract the following structural dimensions:
1. Hook Architecture (How I typically open)
2. Cadence and Line-Break Rhythm (My pacing and whitespace)
3. Insight Density (How much information per paragraph or slide)
4. Visual Cue Timing (Where imagery or text overlays appear)

Provide concrete examples from the text for each dimension.

Before analyzing, ask clarifying questions if the text sample is ambiguous or too short. If you cannot determine a specific dimension from the text provided, say "I don't know" rather than inventing patterns, facts, or citations.
```

**How to adapt:** Paste a transcript or text from your top-performing post into the `[TEXT_SAMPLE]` placeholder.

### 3.9.3 Cross-Platform Translation Analysis

**When to use this:** Use this to compare how a single core idea should be structurally rearchitected for two different platforms.

**Expected output:** A side-by-side structural outline highlighting the distinct narrative choices required for each channel.

```text
Act as a cross-platform editorial director. I need to translate a single pillar insight into two distinct platform-native experiences.

Core Insight: [CORE_INSIGHT]
Platform A: [PLATFORM_A]
Platform B: [PLATFORM_B]

Create a side-by-side structural outline for both platforms. For each, specify:
1. The opening hook strategy.
2. The narrative arc or tension sequence.
3. The closing move or call to action.
4. An annotation explaining why this structure fits the platform's native grammar.

Before outlining, ask clarifying questions if the core insight or target platforms are ambiguous. If you do not know the specific narrative grammar of a platform, say "I don't know" rather than inventing facts, statistics, or citations.
```

**How to adapt:** Replace the `[CORE_INSIGHT]` with the central thesis of your next pillar piece of content.

```text
Act as a content operations manager. I need to codify the rules for a new content format I am testing on [TARGET_PLATFORM].

Here are the details of my recent successful experiment:
[EXPERIMENT_DETAILS]

Draft a Platform-Format Playbook entry that includes:
1. Hook patterns that work for this specific format.
2. Ideal structural length and line-break cadence rules.
3. Visual cue specifications.
4. Tone notes specific to this format.

Before drafting, ask clarifying questions if my experiment details are ambiguous. If you lack specific industry knowledge about my niche, say "I don't know" rather than inventing facts, statistics, or citations.
```

**How to adapt:** Fill in the `[EXPERIMENT_DETAILS]` with the metrics and structural choices of your latest winning post.

### 3.9.5 Summary vs. Rearchitecture Diagnosis

**When to use this:** Use this to diagnose why a repurposed piece of content feels generic or fails to engage on a specific platform.

**Expected output:** A diagnostic breakdown identifying where you defaulted to summary instead of structural rearchitecture.

```text
Act as an editorial diagnostic consultant. I recently repurposed a pillar piece of content for [TARGET_PLATFORM], but it performed poorly and felt generic.

Here is the original pillar insight: [ORIGINAL_INSIGHT]
Here is the repurposed draft I published: [REPURPOSED_DRAFT]

Diagnose the failure by identifying where the draft defaulted to simple summarization rather than structural rearchitecture. Point out missed opportunities for platform-native hooks, cadence shifts, or visual cues. Then, provide a revised structural outline that properly translates the idea.

Before diagnosing, ask clarifying questions if my drafts are ambiguous. If you cannot determine the exact cause without more context, say "I don't know" rather than inventing facts, statistics, or citations.
```

**How to adapt:** Be as specific as possible in the `[REPURPOSED_DRAFT]` placeholder about the structural choices you made.

### 3.9.6 Emerging Platform Grammar Map

**When to use this:** Use this to map the structural grammar of a new or emerging platform you want to test.

**Expected output:** A foundational playbook entry detailing the hooks, rhythms, and visual expectations of a new channel.

```text
Act as a digital trend analyst and content strategist. I am expanding my content ecosystem to a new platform.

The new platform is: [NEW_PLATFORM]
My core niche is: [CORE_NICHE]

Based on the current native grammar of this platform, draft a foundational Playbook entry for my niche. Include:
1. The dominant hook styles and pattern interrupts.
2. The expected narrative rhythm and pacing.
3. The role of visual or audio cues.
4. How my niche can adapt to these expectations without losing authority.

Before drafting, ask clarifying questions if the platform or niche is ambiguous. If you do not know the current algorithmic preferences of this specific platform, say "I don't know" rather than inventing facts, statistics, or citations.
```

**How to adapt:** Change the `[NEW_PLATFORM]` to the specific emerging channel you are investigating.

# 6.9 Discuss: Book Club and Group Prompts

**How to run this session:** This 45-minute book club session uses two warm-up prompts, six core prompts, and two closing prompts. Spend two minutes on warm-ups and closings, and four minutes on core topics. When disagreements arise about triage thresholds, encourage participants to share specific examples of AI drafts that required deeper editing rather than debating abstract quality standards.

### 6.9.1 Warm-up

#### 6.9.1.1 Setup friction

*What specific item on the Sprint Preflight Checklist made you realize how much setup friction currently slows down your own repurposing sessions?*

*Facilitator note:* Start with a quick round-robin to lower the barrier and get everyone sharing their initial reactions.

#### 6.9.1.2 Parallel generation

*Which step in the Parallel Generation Burst feels the most counterintuitive compared to your current habit of editing one platform at a time?*

*Facilitator note:* Invite volunteers to share their answers to build momentum without pressuring quieter members.

### 6.9.2 Core

#### 6.9.2.1 Triage thresholds

*Where might the strict readiness thresholds in the Sprint Triage Framework actually compromise the quality of your content if your audience expects highly polished nuance?*

*Facilitator note:* Listen for whether participants distinguish between structural correctness and the deeper editorial nuance that AI might miss.

#### 6.9.2.2 The ninety-second limit

*What specific mental shift is required to successfully categorize an AI draft within a strict ninety-second limit instead of immediately fixing its flaws?*

*Facilitator note:* Pay attention to whether the group recognizes the difference between assessing readiness and performing actual editing.

#### 6.9.2.3 Tracking overshoots

*How would reviewing your Sprint Triage Log reveal that you consistently overshoot your time budget on specific platforms, and what structural change would fix it?*

*Facilitator note:* Encourage the group to explore how tracking time exposes hidden perfectionism traps during the sprint.

#### 6.9.2.4 When the polish fails

*In what specific scenarios would running the Final Polish Pass checklist actually cause you to fail the sixty-minute time limit due to unexpected AI errors?*

*Facilitator note:* Guide the conversation toward edge cases where prompt drift generates so many flagged items that the sprint breaks down.

#### 6.9.2.5 Scheduling bottlenecks

*What specific logistical bottlenecks in the Scheduling and Archiving process threaten to extend your sixty-minute sprint into a much longer administrative chore?*

*Facilitator note:* Listen for insights on how moving assets to external tools can quietly destroy the time-boxed boundary of the sprint.

#### 6.9.2.6 Cognitive overload

*Why might executing the Parallel Generation Burst simultaneously across multiple tabs overwhelm your computer's memory or your own cognitive load during a live sprint?*

*Facilitator note:* If the discussion stalls, ask the group to share an example of a time technical friction derailed their focus.

### 6.9.3 Closing

#### 6.9.3.1 Blocking the time

*What specific day and time will you block on your calendar to run your first sprint, and what potential interruption must you preemptively guard against?*

*Facilitator note:* Invite volunteers to share their chosen time block to land the session with a concrete commitment.

#### 6.9.3.2 The first preflight

*Which single item from the Sprint Preflight Checklist will you prepare today to ensure your very first sixty-minute session starts without any setup friction?*

*Facilitator note:* Ask participants to name one actionable takeaway to ensure the group leaves with a clear, forward-looking focus.

### 6.9.4 If the group is quiet during Warm-up
- Ask a simple yes/no question about whether they currently edit platforms one by one, then invite elaboration.
- Share a brief, relatable example of a time setup friction derailed your own creative session.

### 6.9.5 If the group is quiet during Core
- Offer a hypothetical scenario where a creator spends twenty minutes perfecting a single LinkedIn post during the sprint.
- Ask the group to identify which of the triage categories they find most difficult to assign quickly.

### 6.9.6 If the group is quiet during Closing
- Ask participants to write down their chosen sprint day on a slip of paper and share it with the person next to them.
- Invite the group to collectively name the biggest benefit of time-boxing their repurposing workflow.
